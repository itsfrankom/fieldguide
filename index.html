An introduction to Santa Fe

It’s a City Different

Spend some time with the excellent Santa Fe County tourism guide and I guarantee you'll be astonished at the culture and creativity that comes out of this area.

Although my personal favorite is the display of antique barbed wire at the Casa Grande Trading Post in Cerrillos, there's such a breadth of experiences here that you and those you travel with could never be bored.

The key word here is travel.

But if you're reading this, chances are you've moved beyond travel and into relocation — or some variation of it.

You're hopefully devouring everything produced by tourism bureaux, real estate agents and countless other organizations. And that's as it should be.

Because Santa Fe may be the City Different for tourists, but for those of us who live here, it's something else entirely.

It's the City Revealing. Perplexing. Exciting. Elusive. Historical. Complex. And so much more.

Why is Santa Fe so unusual among American state capitals?

Well, for a start, it's the oldest, founded in 1610 — although New Mexico didn't become a state until 1912.

And then there are the people. Indigenous communities whose roots here stretch back centuries before the Spanish arrived. Hispanic families whose histories are woven into the region. Europeans, Jewish merchants and traders who followed the Santa Fe Trail and sometimes stayed. Newcomers from every corner of the country and beyond.

All of them have contributed to a place that resists easy description.

So, welcome!

Find your place among the people who came before you. Get out and discover what this extraordinary corner of New Mexico has to offer, from September's Desfile de los Niños Pet Parade to the internationally celebrated Indian Market, International Folk Art Market and Spanish Market.

But don't stop at the attractions.

There's a whole other Santa Fe beyond the visitor guides. It's the place where people live, work, raise families, make friends and occasionally wonder why things are done quite the way they are.

That's the Santa Fe I want to introduce you to.
