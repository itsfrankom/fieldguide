Moving to Santa Fe · Field note 01

What the listings won't tell you

A beautiful house is only the beginning. Here are five practical things worth understanding before you decide where—and how—to live in Santa Fe.

By Frank O'Mahony · November 2026 · Practical relocation guide

Online listings can tell you how many bedrooms a house has. They rarely tell you how the altitude will feel on your first hike, why two properties with similar prices can require very different maintenance, or whether your favorite neighborhood fits your actual weekday routine.

Those questions are not reasons to avoid Santa Fe. They're reasons to arrive informed.

1. The high desert is not a marketing phrase

Santa Fe sits at roughly 7,000 feet above sea level. The elevation, strong sun, dry air and seasonal temperature swings are part of daily life. Visitors may need time to acclimatize; homeowners learn to think about sun exposure, insulation, heating, cooling and water use.

Before you fall for a terrace, visit at different times of day and ask how the home performs in winter as well as summer.

2. Adobe is beautiful. Maintenance still matters.

Santa Fe architecture includes traditional adobe, newer Pueblo Revival construction and many other building types. Similar-looking exteriors can conceal very different materials and maintenance needs.

Ask what the walls and roof are actually made of, when the roof was last serviced, how drainage works, and whether a qualified inspector familiar with local construction has reviewed the property. A flat roof is not automatically a problem—but its condition and maintenance history matter.

3. Your address does not tell the whole water story

Water service and water rights are not the same thing. Depending on the property, you may encounter municipal service, a shared system or a private well. Properties with acreage may raise additional questions about irrigation and legally documented water rights.

Don't assume that buying land automatically conveys permission to use water however you wish. Confirm the water source, associated rights if relevant, and any restrictions with qualified local professionals before closing.

4. Neighborhood choice is a lifestyle decision

The Historic Eastside, Tesuque, Eldorado and other Santa Fe-area communities offer very different combinations of space, convenience, atmosphere and travel time. A map pin can make two homes look close together while the day-to-day experience is quite different.

Spend time in a neighborhood outside the showing appointment. Try the grocery run. Drive your likely commute. Notice the road surface, lighting, winter access and distance to the things you actually do.

5. The commute deserves a test drive

Some buyers work in Santa Fe; others commute toward Los Alamos or work remotely. Routes, weather and schedules can change how a location feels. If a commute is part of your plan, test the actual route at the hour you'd travel rather than relying only on a generic map estimate.

The question behind all five

Not simply “Do I love this house?” but “Will this house and this location work for the life I intend to live?” That's where local knowledge earns its keep.

A note on accuracy: Property conditions, water arrangements and travel circumstances vary. This guide is general information, not an inspection, legal opinion or guarantee about a particular home.
